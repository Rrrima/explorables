# Sit, Stay, Think: explainer script

## Talking points
- The famous breed ranking (Coren 1994) was a survey of 199 obedience judges: Border Collie first, Afghan Hound last (D1).
- Testing animal minds is hard because animals read us (Clever Hans, 1904: right 89% when he could see the questioner, 6% when he couldn't) (H6).
- The ranking measures trainability: under 5 repetitions to learn a command at the top, 80–100+ at the bottom (D2).
- Labs test one skill at a time (pointing, cylinder), and breeds differ much more on some skills than others: self-control 0.70, reading people 0.39, memory 0.17 (D11).
- Breed explains about 9% of an individual dog's behaviour (18,385 dogs) (D7).
- "EQ" is tested in pieces: the eyebrow muscle LAOM that wolves barely have (E15), and 15 of 18 dogs approaching a crying person (E9).
- Cats: hard to test (7 of 26 dropped out). At an unsolvable task 12/14 dogs looked back at a human against 6/14 cats (C1).
- Cats know their names (C2), and 64% of kittens are securely attached, like ~65% of infants (C7). No study ranks cat breeds by intelligence (C15).
- There's no single IQ: ask which skill, and which animal.
