# Promoted — explainer script

About 75 seconds. Style: the crayon drawings of the *Promoted* scrolly: black wax on white, slouchy office workers whose trousers darken with competence, one red crayon for Peter's rule. Source: Pluchino, Rapisarda & Garofalo, "The Peter principle revisited: a computational study", *Physica A* 389 (2010).

## Two-column script

| # | Voiceover | Visuals and motion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In nineteen sixty-nine, Laurence Peter made a joke that stuck: in any hierarchy, people rise to their level of incompetence. | "1969" scrawled big. The quote writes itself; a red worker at the top of a staircase drops a box. |
| 2 | Forty years later, three researchers in Italy built that company inside a computer: a hundred and sixty people, on six floors. | A pyramid of tiny workers builds floor by floor, 81 at the bottom to one boss. "160 people · 6 floors". |
| 3 | Every year, anyone who is bad at their job is fired, anyone over sixty retires, and each empty desk goes to someone from the floor below. | One floor up close: a pale worker drops a box and walks off; another walks off with "60" overhead; someone hops up from below into the empty desk. |
| 4 | Common sense says: promote the best. If you're good at your job, you'll be good at the next one. | A row of workers; the darkest is lifted a floor and keeps their dark trousers. |
| 5 | But the next job is often a different job. A great salesperson can make a terrible sales manager. | A dark-trousered "salesperson" hops into a "sales manager" desk; the trousers drain pale; red question marks. |
| 6 | So the researchers tried a second rule, Peter's rule: after a promotion, your competence starts over, at random. | A promoted worker's trousers flicker through random shades, then settle. "competence redrawn". |
| 7 | Under Peter's rule, promoting the best made the whole company worse. Its efficiency fell by about ten points. | A red crayon line falls from 70% to 60%. "−10 points". |
| 8 | Good workers kept climbing until they landed in a job they were bad at. And there they stayed. | A dark worker climbs a staircase step by step; on the last step the trousers go pale; they slump and sweat. "level of incompetence". |
| 9 | Stranger still, promoting the worst made it better, by about twelve points. | A second line rises to 82%. "+12 points". |
| 10 | Since nobody knows which rule is true, the safest choice was a coin flip: promote the best or the worst, at random. | A coin spins between "best" and "worst". |
| 11 | Sometimes the rule that feels fairest is also the riskiest one. | The staircase again, the red worker at the top. End card. |

## Audio plan

Voice: ElevenLabs. Music: wry office jazz. Effects: pops, footsteps, a box thud, a stamp, a coin flip, a chime.
