# Fibre by Fibre — explainer script

About 70 seconds. Style: the quiet page of *Fibre by Fibre*: washi paper with visible fibres, sumi-ink brush lines that bleed a little, Barlow type, one vermilion seal. Sources: the Fibre by Fibre research report (M, H, R tags).

## Two-column script

| # | Voiceover | Visuals and motion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A sheet of paper is a mat of plant fibres, mostly air, held together by water. | A washi sheet; a round lens slides over it and shows a loose web of fibres with gaps; small water marks join them. |
| 2 | To make it by hand, you soak and beat bark until the fibres come apart, then lift them from a vat on a fine screen. | Bark strips struck by a mallet fray into fibres; a screen dips into a vat and lifts out a wet sheet. |
| 3 | The fibre matters. Kozo, used for Japanese washi, is about ten millimetres long. Eucalyptus, in most copy paper, is under one. | Two brush lines at true scale: kozo 10 mm, eucalyptus 0.9 mm. |
| 4 | Paper began in China, earlier than the usual date of a hundred and five. Fragments survive from the first century before the common era. | A ruler; "105 CE" with a line through it; a fragment mark further left in the 1st century BCE. |
| 5 | It reached Baghdad by seven ninety-four, Spain by ten fifty-six, and Fabriano in Italy, where watermarks began in twelve eighty-two. | An ink line travels west through Baghdad 794, Xàtiva 1056, Fabriano 1282. |
| 6 | In nineteen-oh-one, about seventy thousand Japanese households made washi. By two thousand and nine, two hundred and ninety-five remained. | 700 dots (each one hundred households) go out until three remain. |
| 7 | Meanwhile the world makes more paper than ever: about four hundred million tonnes a year. | Sheets stack up; "≈ 400 million tonnes a year". |
| 8 | Hold a sheet under low, raking light, and its surface appears: fine laid lines from the mould's wires, and heavier chain lines. | A lamp lowers beside a sheet; laid and chain lines rise out of it. |
| 9 | Ink tells the rest. On unsized paper it blooms into a cloud. Sized with glue, it stays where the brush left it. | Two drops: one blooms and feathers, one stays crisp. "unsized" / "sized". |
| 10 | On a screen, we rebuild that surface from noise: layers of it, lit from a low angle, until it reads as paper. | Three tiles: noise, layered noise, lit; they stack into a sheet. |
| 11 | Every sheet keeps a record of its fibre, its mould, and the hands that made it. | A sheet with a vermilion seal. End card. |

## Audio plan

Voice: ElevenLabs. Music: koto and soft piano. Effects: paper rustle, water, mallet, brush, chime.
