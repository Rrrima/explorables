# Lucky — explainer script

About 75 seconds. Style: the lab notebook of the *Lucky* scrolly: graph paper, inked people as black blots, glossy green and red ink balls, Fraunces display type, yellow highlighter. Source: Pluchino, Biondo & Rapisarda, "Talent vs Luck", *Advances in Complex Systems* (2018).

## Two-column script

| # | Voiceover | Visuals and motion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Imagine a thousand people. Each has a different amount of talent, and all of them start with the same ten units of money. | A square world on graph paper fills with 1,000 ink blots, darker for more talent. Labels pop in: "1,000 people", "talent 0.6 ± 0.1", "everyone starts with 10". |
| 2 | Every six months, chance rolls past them. Green balls are lucky breaks. Red balls are bad luck. | Green balls roll in from the edges on "green", red ones on "red", then wander a little each half-year. |
| 3 | When a green ball comes by, talent matters. A talented person is more likely to grab it, and their money doubles. | Close-up on one person. A green ball arrives; a talent gauge spins and lands inside; the blot grows and a coin stack doubles from 10 to 20. |
| 4 | When a red ball comes by, talent doesn't help at all. Their money is cut in half. | Two people, one very talented and one not, both meet a red ball and both shrink; their coin stacks halve. |
| 5 | Run this for forty years, and the money ends up lopsided. The richest fifth hold about eighty percent of it. | The world runs for forty years at speed; blots swell or fade. A bar fills: the richest 20% hold about 80% of all money. |
| 6 | So who ends up on top? Usually not the most talented. In the researchers' own run, the richest person's talent was zero point six one, barely above average. | The talent bell curve. A crown lands at 0.61, just right of the middle. |
| 7 | The most talented person, at zero point eight nine, ended with less than they started with. | A marker at 0.89 in the tail; their coin stack shrinks from 10 to 0.6. |
| 8 | Over ten thousand runs, the winners averaged just zero point six seven. Talent helps. Luck decides. | A second, green curve for the winners of 10,000 runs, centred at 0.67, barely right of everyone's 0.6. |
| 9 | That matters for how we hand out rewards. Give money to whoever is already successful, and you mostly reward the lucky. | Coins stream into the biggest blots, each wearing a little green "lucky" tag. |
| 10 | In the model, giving everyone a small, equal share worked better. It more than doubled the number of talented people who got ahead. | Two bars: no grants 32%, equal shares 69%. |
| 11 | Talent is necessary. It just isn't enough. You also need a few green balls. | One person, a green ball rolling toward them. "talent × luck". End card. |

## Audio plan

Voice: ElevenLabs, one take with word timestamps. Music: playful pizzicato and glockenspiel. Effects: pops, rolls, coin clinks, a ding, a counter tick, a chime.
