# The Half Shell — explainer script

Talking points (from the essay *The Half Shell* and its research report):
- An oyster tastes of the water it grew in (osmoconformer: amino acids, taurine ≈ 80%, T1/T2).
- Oysters were poor people's food (Canal Street Plan, 6¢; C5); NY beds closed by 1927 (C4); today ~$3.34 each (Q17).
- Happy hour: bar pays ~77–80¢ (C1); the $1 oyster loses money once shucking is paid; a loss leader, drinks pay (C1, C2).
- Place: briny north, mild Gulf, cucumber/melon Pacific (T8–T15).
- Season: plump before spawning, milky in summer; Vibrio peaks May–Oct (T1, Q22, Q27).
- Price: older, rarer, bigger; Coffin Bay King 6–7 years, A$100 (Q6, Q8).
- A good one: shut tight, heavy, liquor, smells of the sea, tagged (Q20–Q26).

## Two-column script

| # | Voiceover | Visuals and motion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | An oyster is a little jar of the sea it grew in. Every one on this platter tastes of different water. | The essay's platter on indigo cloth over the sunlit table; oysters set down one by one around the mignonette; tiny water swatches appear beside three of them on "different". |
| 2 | For centuries, oysters were food for the poor. In New York, six cents bought all you could eat. | A newspaper of oysters and a tankard of porter; then a red globe lamp glows and a price card "6¢" swings in on "six cents". |
| 3 | Then sewage closed the beds. The last one in New York shut in nineteen twenty-seven. Now a single oyster costs over three dollars. | A murky bed with a CONDEMNED board ("1927"), then a single oyster on ice with a "$3.34" tag. |
| 4 | So why a dollar at five o'clock? The bar pays about seventy-seven cents for each one. After the shucker is paid, it loses money. | The red clock ticks to five; the tag drops to $1; twenty nickels line up and split: ~77¢ to the crate and sack, the rest slides to the oyster knife. |
| 5 | That's the trick. The cheap oyster fills the seats, and the drinks pay for it. | A martini and a coupe slide in; a pile of coins grows beside them. |
| 6 | The taste comes from survival… Saltier water, more savoury oyster. | Big oyster with a brass magnifier on its cells; the water darkens from brackish to ocean and the cells fill with taurine dots. |
| 7 | So place matters… cucumber and melon. | The North America map (real coastlines): blue dots in the north, butter dots in the Gulf, green dots on the Pacific. |
| 8 | Season matters too… Vibrio bacteria. | The plate-as-calendar: a bead goes round; the centre oyster swells, then turns milky; the red Vibrio arc grows May–October. |
| 9 | The priciest aren't a different animal… a hundred Australian dollars. | True-scale lineup against a 20 cm ruler; the King slides in and dwarfs the rest; "A$100". |
| 10 | And a good one? Shut tight, heavy, full of liquor, smelling of the sea. Ask to see the tag. | A tray of six: one gapes (✕), the rest clamp shut; a full oyster glistens; a harvest tag swings in. |

## Audio plan

Voice: ElevenLabs, speed 0.92, pauses between scenes. Music: gentle acoustic morning café, plucked guitar and soft marimba, no vocals. Effects: ice clink, shell clack, a clock tick, coin clinks, a glass clink, a soft chime at the end.
